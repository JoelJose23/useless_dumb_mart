### Project Description

UselessMart™ is an intentionally terrible e-commerce platform designed to provide
the worst possible online shopping experience while maintaining surprisingly
clean and functional software architecture.

It sells completely useless products, introduces unnecessary obstacles at every
stage of the purchasing process, and somehow still manages to complete the order.

### The Problem (that doesn't exist)

After the rise of AI, all the websites are like robots — perfect, same and boring.

Every button works.
Every checkout process makes sense.
Every interface tries to help the user.

**We decided this was a problem.**

Modern websites have become too predictable, too convenient and far too
concerned about user satisfaction.

Where is the confusion?
Where is the suffering?
Where is the button that runs away when you try to click it?

The internet needed something worse.

### The Solution (that nobody asked for)

Introducing **UselessMart™** — an e-commerce platform that actively works
against its users.

Our platform features:

- Completely useless products
- An unnecessarily difficult academic CAPTCHA
- A secret phrase that lets you bypass the CAPTCHA
- Buttons that literally run away from your cursor
- Reversed scrolling
- Ridiculous checkout fees
- Suspicious payment verification
- Randomly encrypted OTPs
- Fake loading screens
- Completely unnecessary popups
- Toxic motivational messages
- A shopping cart that questions your life choices
- An order that may or may not become a rock
- A refund system designed to make you regret buying anything
- A uselessness score to measure how badly things are going

All of this is wrapped in a clean, functional React application.

Because if we're going to build something useless, **we might as well engineer
the uselessness properly.**

### 🧠 Are You Worthy?™ CAPTCHA

Before adding an item to the cart, users may be subjected to a completely
unnecessary academic assessment.

The system randomly selects questions from different subjects and gives the
user a limited number of attempts.

Because shopping should require educational qualifications.

---

### 🕵️ Secret CAPTCHA Bypass

For users who discover the sacred phrase:

```text
I am Useless
```

The CAPTCHA immediately accepts defeat.

This is intentionally hidden inside the CAPTCHA as an Easter egg.

---

### 🏃 Runaway Checkout Button

The checkout button detects the user's cursor and attempts to escape.

After several attempts, it eventually accepts its fate.

---

### 🔐 Randomly Encrypted OTP

After entering fake payment details, users receive an OTP encoded using a
randomly selected method such as:

* Reverse Cipher
* Binary
* Hexadecimal
* Base64
* Caesar Digit Shift

The OTP itself remains predictable for demonstration purposes.

Because even our security system is mostly theatre.

---

### 💀 Meme Explosion

Wrong CAPTCHA answers trigger an increasingly disappointed collection of
memes and messages.

The system provides emotional feedback that no one requested.

---

### 🪨 Order Transformation

After successfully completing payment, the user discovers what they actually
ordered.

Spoiler:

**It's a rock.**

# Design Philosophy

UselessMart™ follows one simple philosophy:

> **If something can be made unnecessarily complicated, make it unnecessarily complicated.**

However, the underlying software remains intentionally clean.

The project demonstrates that good engineering practices can be applied even
when the product itself is completely ridiculous.

The contrast between:

**professional engineering**

and

**horrific UX decisions**

is the entire point.

---

# Why UselessMart™?

AI has made modern software increasingly efficient.

Interfaces are becoming smarter.
Recommendations are becoming better.
Automation is everywhere.

But somewhere along the way, websites started becoming predictable.

UselessMart™ asks:

> **What happens if we deliberately build the opposite?**

Instead of optimizing for:

* Conversion
* Convenience
* Accessibility
* User satisfaction
* Efficiency

we optimize for:

* Confusion
* Chaos
* Unnecessary steps
* Mild emotional damage
* Maximum uselessness

And somehow...

**it still works.**
